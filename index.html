Adaptive Regolith Compaction System

Preparing lunar regolith for microwave-sintered landing pads

  • Senior Design Team 517
    FAMU-FSU College of Engineering
  • Sponsored by NASA
    Marshall Space Flight Center

Results

Project debrief

When a lander touches down on the Moon, its engine plume blasts loose regolith outward at high speed. Those abrasive particles can scour the lander and any hardware nearby. A hardened landing pad keeps the surface in place.

NASA Marshall Space Flight Center's MMPACT initiative is investigating microwave sintering to turn lunar regolith into durable landing pad surfaces. Before the regolith can be sintered, the surface needs to be prepared.

Team 517 is building an automated compaction tool for that step. It measures regolith hardness as it works and adjusts its vibration frequency in real time, so each area gets only the compaction energy it needs. The goal is to limit regolith abrasion and reduce wear on lander systems similar to HLS architectures.
